Part X

Semantic Repair Descent and the SAGA Comparison Theorem

Build two ways of measuring brokenness — one in the language of repairs, one in the language of equations — without either referring to the other. Part X proves that they carry the same scale: the two residual classes coincide under a natural \( H^1 \) isomorphism, and under a true sheaf condition the zero class is equivalent to an actual global repair.

1. Fixed objects and the central statement

Fix the AAT site \( S_X \) of an architecture object, a context \( W \), and a finite AAT cover \( \mathcal{U} = \{ u_i : U_i \to W \}_{i \in I} \) in which every leg is a monomorphism — a monomorphic AAT cover. A total order on the index set gives ordered overlaps \( U_{ij} \) and \( U_{ijk} \); empty pullbacks are removed from the products and their values fixed by an empty-overlap normalization. In the finite-meet poset model of Part II every morphism is a monomorphism, so the assumption is automatic there; on a general context category it is an explicit hypothesis.

Part X keeps three ledgers strictly apart:

Selected
The cover and normalization; the semantic atom system, repair support, local repair relations, and local repair atlas; the equation system \( E \) and its local equation-lift atlas; the atom/equation interpretation, the local-state interpretation \( \beta \), the completeness conditions, and the true sheaf condition used for global gluing.
Generated
\( M_{\mathrm{sem}} \), \( Q_E \), the two Čech complexes, the residuals \( r_{\mathrm{sem}} \) and \( r_E \), the coefficient map \( \Phi \), and the cochain map \( \kappa \).
Proved
Repair-relation soundness, SAGA presentation exactness discharge, the isomorphy of \( \Phi \), differential commutation, cocycle / coboundary preservation, the \( H^1 \) isomorphism, the residual class correspondence, and the equivalence of the zero class with an actual global repair.
Theorem X.1.1

SAGA Central Theorem

Lean: proved

On a monomorphic AAT cover \( \mathcal{U} \), fix: (1) the abelian-group-valued presheaf \( M_{\mathrm{sem}} \) generated from semantic atoms, projection, repair support, and restriction-stable local repair relations; (2) the presheaf \( Q_E \) generated from the architectural equation system \( E \) by Part III, Theorem 11.4; (3) a restriction-natural linear map sending supported semantic atoms to displayed equation residual classes; (4) relation completeness and equation-generator completeness of that map, checked per cover intersection (relation soundness is derived from the local-state data); (5–7) an affine semantic repair system \( P_{\mathrm{sem}} \) with its selected atlas, an equation-lift system \( P_E \) with its selected atlas, and a restriction-natural, generator-equivariant map \( \beta : P_{\mathrm{sem}} \to P_E \); (8) the empty-overlap normalization. Then:

\[ \Phi : M_{\mathrm{sem}} \xrightarrow{\ \sim\ } Q_E, \qquad \kappa^{\bullet} : C^{\bullet}_{\mathrm{sem}}(\mathcal{U}) \xrightarrow{\ \sim\ } C^{\bullet}_E(\mathcal{U}), \qquad \kappa_{*} : H^1_{\mathrm{sem}}(\mathcal{U}) \xrightarrow{\ \sim\ } \check{H}^1(\mathcal{U}, Q_E), \]

with \( \kappa \) commuting with the differentials, and for the residuals generated as differences of the two selected atlases, \( \kappa_{*}([r_{\mathrm{sem}}]) = [r_E] \). If moreover the semantic repair object is a true sheaf on the AAT topology and \( \mathcal{U} \) is a cover of that topology, then

\[ \mathrm{Nonempty}\, P_{\mathrm{sem}}(W) \iff [r_{\mathrm{sem}}] = 0 \iff [r_E] = 0. \]

Independence
No equation appears in the construction of the semantic side; no repair appears in the construction of the equation side. The two coefficients, complexes, and residuals are generated separately, and their agreement is the content of the theorem — never an input.

2. The cover-relative Čech complex

For any abelian-group-valued presheaf \( F \) on the cover intersection diagram, the three-term complex is 

C^0 = prod_i           F(U_i)
C^1 = prod_{i<j}       F(U_ij)     (nonempty overlaps)
C^2 = prod_{i<j<k}     F(U_ijk)    (nonempty triples)

(delta^0 p)_{ij} = p_j|_{U_ij} - p_i|_{U_ij}
(delta^1 r)_{ijk} = r_{jk}| - r_{ik}| + r_{ij}|

Lemma X.2.1A (monomorphic-cover normalization) shows that with monomorphic legs, self-overlaps and reversed-order overlaps carry canonical equalities, so the increasing-index model recovers the descent data of all ordered overlaps; with \( \delta^1 \delta^0 = 0 \) (Lemma X.2.2), the cover-relative \( H^1(\mathcal{U}, F) = Z^1 / B^1 \) is defined (Definition X.2.3). Both sides of SAGA use this same construction — applied to two independently generated coefficients.

3. Coefficients generated from semantic atoms

A semantic repair presentation (Definition X.3.1) places, on each local context, the supported semantic atoms and a family of restriction-stable local repair relations. The semantic coefficient (Definition X.3.2) is the quotient of the free abelian group on supported atoms by the subgroup the relations generate: 

F_sem(V) = free abelian group on supported semantic atoms
R_rep(V) = subgroup generated by local repair relations
M_sem(V) = F_sem(V) / R_rep(V)

Proposition X.3.3 proves \( M_{\mathrm{sem}} \) is a presheaf — restriction descends to the quotient because the relations are restriction-stable — and Definition X.3.4 forms the semantic repair complex \( C^{\bullet}_{\mathrm{sem}}(\mathcal{U}) \) by applying Section 2 to \( M_{\mathrm{sem}} \). Nothing on this side mentions the equation system.

4. Residuals generated from semantic mismatch

An affine semantic repair system (Definition X.4.1) lets repair words act on local repair states; relation soundness, stabilizer completeness, and local transitivity make each nonempty local state set an \( M_{\mathrm{sem}} \)-torsor. Choosing a local repair \( p_i \) on each chart — the semantic local repair atlas (Definition X.4.2) — the pairwise difference on each nonempty overlap is a unique coefficient element: 

p_j|_{U_ij} = r_sem,ij + p_i|_{U_ij}

Lemma X.4.3 proves \( r_{\mathrm{sem}} \) is a 1-cocycle; Theorem X.4.4 proves the class \( [r_{\mathrm{sem}}] \in H^1_{\mathrm{sem}}(\mathcal{U}) \) does not depend on the choice of atlas; and Corollary X.4.5 extracts, from \( [r_{\mathrm{sem}}] = 0 \), a correction \( a \) with \( r_{\mathrm{sem}} = \delta^0 a \) whose corrected family \( p_i^{\mathrm{corr}} = (-a_i) + p_i \) is a matching family on all overlaps.

5. The equation-generated side

Independently, the equation-generated coefficient (Definition X.5.1) is the obstruction quotient \( Q_E = \mathcal{O}_E / I_{\mathrm{Ob}}^E \) produced from the equation system by Part III, Theorem 11.4, restricted to the cover intersection diagram; the geometric Čech complex \( C^{\bullet}_E(\mathcal{U}) \) (Definition X.5.2) applies Section 2 to it. The \( Q_E \)-controlled equation lift problem (Definition X.5.3) selects an equation-lift system \( P_E \) on which \( Q_E \) acts, with its own selected local lift atlas; Lemma X.5.4 gives the geometric cocycle \( r_E \) and its choice independence, exactly parallel to Section 4 — with no reference to repairs.

6. Primary comparison core and equation realization

The bridge data is deliberately thin. The primary SAGA correspondence (Definition X.6.1) is one restriction-natural linear map from supported semantic atoms to displayed equation residual classes; the primary state correspondence (Definition X.6.1B) is one restriction-natural, generator-equivariant map \( \beta : P_{\mathrm{sem}} \to P_E \). SAGA presentation exactness (Definition X.6.2) demands, per intersection, that the repair relations match the kernel and the equation generators are hit — and Lemma X.6.2A proves relation soundness from the state data rather than assuming it.

Theorem X.6.3

Coefficient Presentation Theorem

Lean: proved

From SAGA presentation exactness against any target coefficient \( Q \), the induced map \( \Phi : M_{\mathrm{sem}} \to Q \) is a natural isomorphism on the cover intersection diagram. No inverse and no degreewise complex equivalence is taken as input; Corollary X.6.7 instantiates the target to \( Q_E \), so \( M_{\mathrm{sem}} \simeq Q_E \) is constructed, not postulated.

Claim boundary
Example X.6.6 shows the exactness conditions are material: dropping relation completeness or generator completeness breaks the isomorphism on explicit finite data.

7. Cochain comparison and the SAGA \( H^1 \) isomorphism

Applying \( \Phi \) degreewise gives the comparison map \( \kappa^{\bullet} \) (Definition X.7.1); Theorem X.7.2 proves it commutes with the two independently built differentials, Corollary X.7.3 that it preserves and reflects cocycles and coboundaries, and Theorem X.7.4 that it induces the additive isomorphism \( \kappa_{*} : H^1_{\mathrm{sem}}(\mathcal{U}) \simeq \check{H}^1(\mathcal{U}, Q_E) \).

Theorems X.7.5–7.6

Residual Correspondence and the SAGA Comparison Theorem

Lean: proved

Theorem X.7.5: for the residuals generated from the two selected atlases, \( \kappa_{*}([r_{\mathrm{sem}}]) = [r_E] \) — when the equation atlas is chosen through \( \beta \) the equality holds at the cochain level, and for an independently chosen atlas the difference is an explicit \( \delta_E^0 \)-image. Theorem X.7.6 assembles Sections 2–7 into the comparison half of the central theorem: coefficient isomorphism, cochain commutation, \( H^1 \) isomorphism, and residual class correspondence, each proved in that order.

8. True sheaf descent and actual global repair

A true semantic repair sheaf (Definition X.8.1) is a \( P_{\mathrm{sem}} \) satisfying the sheaf condition for every cover of the selected topology, with a restriction-compatible, locally free and transitive \( M_{\mathrm{sem}} \)-action, \( \mathcal{U} \) a cover of that topology, and subsingleton values on the removed empty intersections. No per-cover amalgamation map is placed as separate data.

Theorem X.8.2 / Corollary X.8.3

Grounded Global Gluing and the equation-side lift

Lean: proved

Under the setting of Theorem X.7.6 with a true semantic repair sheaf:

\[ \mathrm{Nonempty}\, P_{\mathrm{sem}}(W) \iff [r_{\mathrm{sem}}] = 0 \iff [r_E] = 0, \]

and from the correction \( a \) obtained from the zero class, the corrected family \( p_i^{\mathrm{corr}} = (-a_i) + p_i \) is glued by sheaf amalgamation to a unique global section restricting to it. Corollary X.8.3 adds: when \( \beta \) is locally bijective from the exactness data and \( P_E \) is also a sheaf, the base map \( \beta_W \) is a bijection, so a global equation lift exists iff a global semantic repair exists iff \( [r_E] = 0 \).

What is unique
Unique is the amalgamation of the fixed corrected matching family; \( P_{\mathrm{sem}}(W) \) as a whole is not claimed to be a subsingleton — differences of global repairs are carried by degree-zero data.
Separation of regimes
Principle X.8.4: the \( H^1 \) comparison is an additive theorem; nonabelian torsors, 2-cocycles / gerbes / \( H^2 \), and stack descent each require independent statements and are not derived from it.

9. Theorem dependencies

The logical dependencies of Part X are:

Part III:
  E -> Q_E and residual restriction naturality

Part IV:
  cover-relative Cech construction and additive torsor reading

semantic primary data:
  atoms + support + local relations -> M_sem and r_sem

SAGA presentation exactness:
  M_sem -> Q_E -> cochain comparison -> H^1 comparison

true sheaf condition:
  zero class -> corrected matching family -> actual global section

The dependency graph runs one way: the comparison never feeds back into the construction of either side, and the central theorem consumes the sections in the order they are proved.

10. Finite executable realization

Definition X.10.1 fixes the finite SAGA presentation matrix — the finite data from which every object of the theorem can be computed — and Example X.10.2 runs the independently generated circle comparison: an actual 4-chart monomorphic cover whose semantic side generates \( M_{\mathrm{sem}} \cong \mathbb{F}_2 \) from one atom and the relation \( 2\sigma = 0 \), whose equation side generates \( Q_E = \mathbb{Z}/(2) \) from the coordinate \( 2 \), and whose residual classes are nonzero on both sides with \( \kappa_{*}([r_{\mathrm{sem}}]) = [r_E] \) (Appendix B.9).

11. The name, and the conclusion of Part X

The name SAGA is an homage to GAGA — Serre’s comparison between algebraic and analytic geometry — transposed to the comparison between the semantic repair reading and the equation-generated reading of the same architecture. It has nothing to do with the distributed-transaction Saga pattern.

Part X closes the central loop of AAT. Two measures of brokenness are generated independently — one from what can be repaired, one from what equations fail — and proved to carry the same scale: 

semantic atoms + relations  ->  M_sem  ->  r_sem
equation system E           ->  Q_E    ->  r_E

exactness (checked)  ->  Phi  ->  kappa  ->  H^1 isomorphism
kappa_*([r_sem]) = [r_E]

true sheaf  ->  zero class  <->  actual global repair

Terms

Monomorphic AAT cover
A finite AAT cover whose legs are monomorphisms; with the empty-overlap normalization, the increasing-index Čech model recovers all ordered descent data.
Semantic coefficient \( M_{\mathrm{sem}} \)
The quotient presheaf of the free abelian group on supported semantic atoms by the local repair relations.
Equation coefficient \( Q_E \)
The obstruction quotient generated from the equation system by Part III, Theorem 11.4.
Semantic / equation residual
The 1-cocycles generated as pairwise differences of the selected local repair atlas and the selected local lift atlas; their classes are atlas-independent.
SAGA presentation exactness
The per-intersection generator/relation conditions under which the primary correspondence induces \( M_{\mathrm{sem}} \simeq Q_E \).
True semantic repair sheaf
The sheaf-and-torsor condition under which the zero class is equivalent to an actual, uniquely amalgamated global repair.
