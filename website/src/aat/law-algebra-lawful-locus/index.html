Law Algebra, Obstruction Ideal, and the Lawful Locus

Law is read as equation, obstruction as ideal, lawfulness as vanishing.

Research question

Can architectural laws be read as equations on a geometric object, so that lawfulness becomes the vanishing of an ideal rather than a checklist of natural-language principles?

Part II built the architecture geometry as a site with a sheaf category: 

ArchitectureObject A_s^r
  -> ArchitectureGeometry X_s^(r,J)
  -> AATSite(S,V,E,Sig,R,Ov)
  -> AATSh(A_s^r,E,J)

A site and a sheaf category are a stage for local data and gluing, but they are not yet algebraic geometry. The theory becomes algebraic geometry when the geometric object carries a sheaf of commutative rings. Part III supplies that ring: it constructs the law algebra sheaf \(\mathcal{O}_X^U\), collects equation failure into the obstruction ideal sheaf \(\mathcal{I}_{\mathrm{Ob}}^U\), and cuts out the lawful locus as the zero set of that ideal. The central diagram of Part III is 

AATSh(A,E,J)
  -> O_X^U
  -> I_Ob^U subset O_X^U
  -> Flat_U(X) = V(I_Ob^U)
  -> affine AAT charts
  -> StandardArchitectureScheme

Under this structure, a law is not a design slogan. It is an equation, or a family of equations, on the architecture geometry.

From Part II to Part III

The sheaf category obtained in Part II is \(\mathrm{AATSh}(A,U,J) = \mathrm{Sh}(\mathrm{ArchCtx}(A), J_U)\). On this stage, each context \(W\) is first assigned a commutative ring of architecture coordinates readable on \(W\):

\[ W \;\longmapsto\; \mathcal{O}_{\mathrm{raw}}^U(W). \]

A context morphism \(i : W' \to W\) induces a restriction homomorphism of commutative rings \(\mathrm{res}_i : \mathcal{O}_{\mathrm{raw}}^U(W) \to \mathcal{O}_{\mathrm{raw}}^U(W')\). Sheafifying this presheaf yields the sheaf of commutative rings on the AAT site:

\[ \mathcal{O}_X^U \;=\; \bigl(\mathcal{O}_{\mathrm{raw}}^U\bigr)^{+}. \]

The pair \(\bigl(\mathrm{AATSh}(A,U,J),\, \mathcal{O}_X^U\bigr)\) is the ringed AAT topos of Definition III.9.1 below. Everything in Part III — witness ideals, obstruction ideals, affine charts, the lawful locus, and the architecture scheme — lives on this ringed foundation.

Structure sheaf of architecture geometry

Definition III.2.1 (Law Algebra Sheaf). For an architecture geometry \(X = X_C^{V,E,J,k}\), the law algebra sheaf \(\mathcal{O}_X^U\) is the sheaf of commutative rings on the AAT site obtained by sheafifying the observable presheaf of the equation system (equivalently, its presented ambient form): \(\mathcal{O}_X^U \in \mathrm{Sh}(\mathrm{ArchCtx}(A), J_{E,R,\mathrm{Ov}};\ \mathrm{CommAlg}_k)\). For each context \(W\), the section ring \(\mathcal{O}_X^U(W)\) is the commutative ring generated by the architecture coordinates readable on \(W\).

This ring is the function algebra for reading Atoms, signatures, state, effects, semantic facts, and runtime interactions as coordinates. The division of labor is exact: 

Atom is ontology.
Coordinate is a reading of Atom.
Ring is algebra of such readings.

Principle III.2.2 (Commutative Base). The base ring that cuts out the lawful locus is commutative. A coefficient ring \(k \in \mathrm{CommRing}\) is fixed, and each coordinate algebra is read as a commutative \(k\)-algebra: \(\mathcal{O}_X^U(W) \in \mathrm{CommAlg}_k\). Changing \(k\) changes the readings of cancellation, rank, dimension, and derived tensor.

State transition, effect composition, runtime interaction, and operation algebras may be noncommutative. The lawful locus, however, is defined as a zero set, and the coordinate ring that carries zero sets is taken commutative first. Noncommutative structure lives over this base: 

noncommutative operation
  may exist over
commutative law-coordinate geometry.

Noncommutative operations are read as modules, algebra objects, or operation sheaves over the commutative structure sheaf, or as later extensions.

Architectural coordinates

Definition III.3.1 (Coordinate). An architecture coordinate on a context \(W\) is a function \(c : \text{local architecture data on } W \to R\) that reads an Atom configuration, or a derived structure, as a value in a selected coefficient ring \(R\).

Typical coordinate families are atom, signature, law witness, state, effect, authority, semantic, runtime, and boundary coordinates. A coordinate reads structures that were already constructed from the Atom axioms of Part I; it does not create them.

Example III.3.2 (Atom Coordinate). An atom coordinate \(x_a(W)\) reads whether, or with which weight, an Atom \(a\) appears on \(W\). Instances include \(x_{\mathrm{component}}(C)\), \(x_{\mathrm{relation}}(A,B)\), \(x_{\mathrm{capability}}(C,\kappa)\), \(x_{\mathrm{effect}}(e)\), \(x_{\mathrm{authority}}(\mathrm{actor},\mathrm{action},\mathrm{resource})\), \(x_{\mathrm{semantic}}(t,s)\), and \(x_{\mathrm{runtime}}(u,v,h)\).

Example III.3.3 (Signature Coordinate). A signature coordinate reads the value of a selected signature axis: \(\mathrm{sig}_{\mathrm{cycle}}\), \(\mathrm{sig}_{\mathrm{projection}}\), \(\mathrm{sig}_{\mathrm{substitution}}\), \(\mathrm{sig}_{\mathrm{state\ transition}}\), \(\mathrm{sig}_{\mathrm{effect}}\), \(\mathrm{sig}_{\mathrm{authority}}\), \(\mathrm{sig}_{\mathrm{semantic}}\), \(\mathrm{sig}_{\mathrm{runtime}}\), and \(\mathrm{sig}_{\mathrm{boundary}}\). A vanishing signature coordinate is a zero condition on the selected axis, and only on it: 

selected zero != total zero
unselected axis != zero
unmeasured axis != zero

Example III.3.4 (Semantic Coordinate). Following the Semantic Atom of Part I, a semantic coordinate \(\mathrm{sem}_{\Gamma,R,\rho}(t,s)\) is relativized to a UseContext \(\Gamma\), a UseRuleSet \(R\), and a resolution \(\rho\). Agreement of semantic coordinates means agreement of use-rules inside a fixed language game, not agreement of strings or names.

Ambient law algebra

Definition III.4.1 (Free Typed Commutative Algebra). Write \(\mathrm{Coord}_X(W)\) for the set of architecture coordinates visible on \(W\). The free typed commutative algebra over the coefficient ring \(k\) is \(\mathrm{FreeCommAlg}_k(\mathrm{Coord}_X(W))\) — intuitively, the polynomial ring \(k[x_{\mathrm{atom}}, x_{\mathrm{signature}}, x_{\mathrm{state}}, x_{\mathrm{effect}}, x_{\mathrm{authority}}, x_{\mathrm{semantic}}, x_{\mathrm{runtime}}, x_{\mathrm{boundary}}]\) in the coordinates readable on \(W\).

Definition III.4.2 (Structural Relation). Independently of laws, coordinates carry structural relations \(\mathrm{Rel}_{\mathrm{struct}}(W)\): typing relations, source identity consistency, projection and restriction compatibility, boundary incidence, semantic resolution compatibility, state coordinate typing, and runtime trace typing. Structural relations are not there to erase law failure; they make the coordinates cohere as readings of one architecture object. The basic object is the ideal they generate, \(J_{\mathrm{struct}}(W) \subset \mathrm{FreeCommAlg}_k(\mathrm{Coord}_X(W))\), and the text distinguishes the relation polynomials \(\mathrm{Rel}_{\mathrm{struct}}(W)\) from the generated ideal \(J_{\mathrm{struct}}(W)\).

Definition III.4.3 (Presented Ambient Law Algebra). The presented ambient law algebra on \(W\) is the quotient

\[ \mathcal{O}_{\mathrm{raw}}^E(W) \;=\; \mathrm{FreeCommAlg}_k(\mathrm{Coord}_X(W)) \,/\, J_{\mathrm{struct}}(W), \]

used as an affine presentation of the observable ring \(\mathcal{O}_E(W)\) of the equation system: for each context a \(k\)-algebra isomorphism \(\mathrm{present}_{E,W} : \mathcal{O}_{\mathrm{raw}}^E(W) \cong \mathcal{O}_E(W)\) is selected, commuting with context restriction and carrying the equation coordinate labels to the actual coordinates, \(\mathrm{present}_{E,W}(\mathrm{raw}\_\nu_{W,i,a}) = \nu_{W,i,a}\) and \(\mathrm{present}_{E,W}(\mathrm{raw}\_\epsilon_{W,A,i,a}) = \epsilon_{W,A,i,a}\). The affine presentation therefore injects no coordinate family separate from \(E\). Crucially, equation witness ideals are not set to zero at the ambient stage. If every law were quotiented out from the start, the obstruction ideal generated by equation failure would become invisible. Part III therefore separates two roles: 

structural relations:
  coordinate system itself must satisfy them.

equation witness ideals:
  they cut out lawful locus as zeros.

The structure sheaf is the sheafification \(\mathcal{O}_X^U = (\mathcal{O}_E)^{+} \cong (\mathcal{O}_{\mathrm{raw}}^E)^{+}\).

Condition III.4.4 (Restriction-Stable Structural Relations). For \(\mathcal{O}_{\mathrm{raw}}^E\) to be a presheaf of commutative \(k\)-algebras, the structural relation ideal must be stable under context restriction: for each \(i : W' \to W\), the typed coordinate restriction induces \(\mathrm{res}_i : \mathrm{FreeCommAlg}_k(\mathrm{Coord}_X(W)) \to \mathrm{FreeCommAlg}_k(\mathrm{Coord}_X(W'))\) with \(\mathrm{res}_i\bigl(J_{\mathrm{struct}}(W)\bigr) \subset J_{\mathrm{struct}}(W')\). Under this restriction-stability, \(\mathrm{res}_i\) descends to the quotients \(\mathcal{O}_{\mathrm{raw}}^E(W) \to \mathcal{O}_{\mathrm{raw}}^E(W')\).

Condition III.4.5 (Presentation-Stable AAT Site). To use the sheafified sections \(\mathcal{O}_X^U(W)\) as the coordinate algebra of the same local configuration functor, the selected finite presentation must survive sheafification: if the canonical maps \(\mathcal{O}_{\mathrm{raw}}^E(W) \to \mathcal{O}_E(W) \to \mathcal{O}_X^U(W)\) preserve the selected coordinate generators, structural relations, and local functor representation, the AAT site is called presentation-stable. Without this condition \(\mathcal{O}_X^U\) is still a sheaf of commutative rings, but reading \(\mathrm{Spec}_{\mathrm{AAT}}(\mathcal{O}_X^U(W), D_W^U)\) as the affine moduli chart of the raw local configuration functor requires presentation-stability to be stated explicitly.

Principle III.4.6 (Law Does Not Create Coordinates). The equation system \(E\) specifies the equation indices, roles, \(\nu\), and \(\epsilon\); the violation-witness support comes from the equation reading, the signature axes from the signature reading, and the supports demanded on covers from \( \mathrm{Req}_R \) of Part II. But a law is not the ground of existence of Atoms or coordinates: 

law does not create atoms
law does not create coordinates
law cuts out loci

Law as equation

Definition III.5.1 (Equation Coordinate Families). Fix the architectural equation system \(E\) of Part I. For an equation index \(i\), context \(W\), atom \(a\), and architecture object \(A\), two restriction-compatible coordinate families live in the observable ring \(\mathcal{O}_E(W)\): the symbolic violation coordinate \(\nu_{W,i,a}\), which generates the witness ideal, and the object-dependent equation residual \(\epsilon_{W,A,i,a}\), which decides fulfillment on \(A\). To track finite circuits and visibility on covers, a selected violation-witness support \(\mathrm{Viol}_E(i,W) \subset At\) records witness provenance — it does not truncate the generator family of the standard witness ideal, which ranges over all of \(At\). A cycle equation contributes cycle witness atoms, an authority equation authority/effect mismatch atoms, a semantic equation use-rule mismatch atoms. The two families do not share a role: the class of \(\nu_{W,i,a}\) is always zero in the quotient by the ideal it generates, so displayed failure classes use \(\epsilon_{W,A,i,a}\).

Definition III.5.2 (Equation Witness Ideal). The local witness ideal of equation index \(i\) is generated by the symbolic violation coordinates over all atoms:

\[ I_i^E(W) \;=\; \bigl\langle\, \nu_{W,i,a} \;\big|\; a \in At \,\bigr\rangle \;\subset\; \mathcal{O}_E(W). \]

Inside the ambient ring \(I_i^E(W)\) need not be the zero ideal. Restriction compatibility of \(\nu\) gives \(\mathrm{res}_j(I_i^E(W)) \subset I_i^E(W')\) by a generator-level computation, so the context-wise ideals form an ideal subpresheaf \(I_i^E \subset \mathcal{O}_E\); the image of \((I_i^E)^{+} \to (\mathcal{O}_E)^{+} = \mathcal{O}_X^U\) is the equation witness ideal sheaf \(\mathcal{I}_i^E \subset \mathcal{O}_X^U\). This ideal-theoretic encoding is the body of equation failure.

Definition III.5.3 (Displayed Equation Residual). The displayed defect is not free extra data; it is defined from the equation system's residual: \( d_E(W,A,i,a) := \epsilon_{W,A,i,a} \). Equation fulfillment immediately gives \( \mathrm{EquationHolds}_E(i,A) \rightarrow d_E(W,A,i,a) = 0 \rightarrow d_E(W,A,i,a) \in I_i^E(W) \) — the last membership because every ideal contains zero — while \( d_E(W,A,i,a) \) need not be a generator of the witness ideal. This distinction keeps quotient classes from being zero by definition, so a nonzero class is read as a one-way detector of selected equation failure. Ideal-side satisfaction along a point \(p \in \mathrm{Spec}_{\mathrm{AAT}}(\mathcal{O}_E(W), D_W^E)\) or a section \(s\) is \( I_i^E(W) \subset p \) or \( s^{*}_{\mathrm{ideal}} \mathcal{I}_i^E = 0 \); in an \(E\)-generated scheme realization, its equivalence with equation fulfillment is obtained from the generator-level proof of Theorem III.5.2C.

Theorem III.5.2C — Certified bounded inference

Generated Section-Ideal Comparison

Lean: proved

Fix an \(E\)-generated scheme realization: the standard equation-scheme constructor builds, from \(E\), its observable presheaf, and the \(\nu\)-generated ideals, the scheme \(X_E\), the chart functor, the sheafification unit, and a base-change-stable section reading, together with the named producer theorems (residualRegular_E, residualRepresentable_E, witnessIdealLocalizes_E, witnessIdealChart_E, witnessIdealQuasiCoherent_E). Then for every equation index \(i\) and section \(s : T \to X_E\):

\[ \mathrm{EquationHolds}_E(i;s) \iff s^{*}_{\mathrm{ideal}} \mathcal{I}_i^E = 0 \iff s \text{ factors through } V(\mathcal{I}_i^E). \]

Proof reading
The chart family is jointly covering, so vanishing of the extension ideal is equivalent to the vanishing of every local generator evaluation \( \mathrm{ev}_{s,W}(\nu_{W,i,a}) \); the producer theorems' generator equality turns that into the vanishing of every residual \( \mathrm{ev}_{s,W}(\epsilon_{W,A_s,i,a}) \), which is the definition of fulfillment. The correspondence is never a premise; it is proved in the order sheafified generator, section evaluation, producer theorem, ideal generation.

If the selected scheme regime cannot construct the producer theorems, the correspondence between equation fulfillment and ideal vanishing remains unproved and the theorem is not applied. The realization ideal \( \langle \nu - \epsilon \rangle \) and the witness ideal \( \langle \nu \rangle \) are distinct stages: killing the former does not kill \(\nu\) itself.

Representative displayed residuals are \(d_{\mathrm{cycle}}\), \(d_{\mathrm{projection}}\), \(d_{\mathrm{substitution}}\), \(d_{\mathrm{state\ transition}}\), \(d_{\mathrm{effect\ replay}}\), \(d_{\mathrm{authority}}\), \(d_{\mathrm{semantic}}\), \(d_{\mathrm{runtime}}\), and \(d_{\mathrm{interaction}}\).

The semantic defect is read against a fixed language game: it witnesses a mismatch of use-rules, not a mismatch of spelling.

Example III.5.5 (Defect Encoding). With a fixed coefficient ring \(k\) and selected witness family, the auxiliary ideal generated by the selected cycle witness coordinates is

\[ I_{\mathrm{cycle}}^{E,\mathrm{sel}}(W) \;=\; \bigl\langle\, \nu_{W,\mathrm{cycle},c} \;\big|\; c \in \mathrm{CycleWitness}_E(W) \,\bigr\rangle \;\subset\; \mathcal{O}_E(W). \]

Then \( \mathrm{ev}_s(I_{\mathrm{cycle}}^{E,\mathrm{sel}}(W)) = 0 \), or \( I_{\mathrm{cycle}}^{E,\mathrm{sel}}(W) \subset p \), reads that every selected cycle witness vanishes along the section or point. The standard witness ideal \( I_{\mathrm{cycle}}^E(W) = \langle\, \nu_{W,\mathrm{cycle},a} \mid a \in At \,\rangle \) is generated over all atoms per Definition III.5.2, so the selected ideal may be identified with the standard one only when each \( \nu_{W,\mathrm{cycle},a} \) outside the support already belongs to it — vanishing outside the support is the sufficient condition. Only under a fixed no-cancellation discipline may the representative \( g_{\mathrm{cycle}}(W) = \sum_{c} \nu_{W,\mathrm{cycle},c} \) be used, because the sum of witnesses may cancel in positive characteristic or with signed coefficients; selected-family satisfaction is therefore placed on the ideal, not on \( g_{\mathrm{cycle}} = 0 \). The substitution defect is encoded the same way from contract mismatch witness coordinates. The symbolic witness ideal and the object-dependent residual come from the same equation system: the former is generated from \(\nu\), the latter selected from \(\epsilon\).

Principle III.5.6 (No-Cancellation Discipline). Reading witness vanishing in a coordinate algebra requires a no-cancellation discipline on the coefficient system and the encoding. The safe basic forms are Boolean evaluation \(\mathrm{ev}_s(\nu_{W,i,a}) \in \lbrace 0,1 \rbrace\) for selected \((i,a)\), monomial witness ideals \( I_i^E(W) = \langle\, \nu_{W,i,a} \mid a \in At \,\rangle \) (with a finite display of the nonzero generator support in the selected finite regime), and product witnesses such as \(c_{ABC} = e_{AB}\, e_{BC}\, e_{CA}\). For example: 

I_cycle = < c_ABC >
I_auth  = < effect_capture * (1 - has_authority) >
I_sub   = < substitutes_Impl_Base * contract_mismatch >

Under this discipline, a zero witness ideal keeps the meaning "the selected violation witnesses vanished" and additive cancellation is never misread as lawfulness.

Principle III.5.7 (Equation Relativity). A law equation is relativized to the Atom vocabulary \(V\), the architectural equation system \(E\), the coverage topology \(J\), the selected witness family, the selected signature axes, the UseContext \(\Gamma\), the UseRuleSet \(R\), and the resolution \(\rho\). Consequently \(\epsilon_{W,A,i,a} = 0\) within the selected reading asserts nothing about readings that were not selected.

Idempotent collapse and the Stanley-Reisner regime

Two structural results make the Boolean-flavored witness algebra fully computable: idempotent coordinates collapse to a product of copies of \(k\), and square-free witness regimes identify the obstruction ideal with a Stanley-Reisner ideal.

Lemma III.5.6A

Idempotent Coordinate Collapse

Lean: proved
Statement
For finitely many selected coordinates over a field \(k\), let \(A = k[x_v \mid v \in E] \,/\, \langle x_v^2 - x_v \mid v \in E \rangle\). Then \(A\) is a finite product of copies of \(k\), and \(\mathrm{Tor}_i^A(M,N) = 0\) for \(i > 0\), \(I/I^2 = 0\) for every ideal \(I \subset A\), and \(\Omega_{A/k} = 0\).

Finite coordinate set \(E\); \(k\) a field; idempotency relations \(x_v^2 = x_v\) imposed on all selected coordinates.
Boundary
The collapse holds for the idempotent quotient algebra; it is not a claim about the ambient polynomial ring or non-Boolean coefficient regimes.

Definition III.5.6B (Square-Free Witness Regime). On a context \(W\), fix a finite set of selected primitive witness coordinates \(E_W = \lbrace e_1, \dots, e_n \rbrace\) and keep the deformation side of the coordinate algebra as \(k[e_1, \dots, e_n]\). The context \(W\) is in a square-free witness regime when the equation system's law reading selects a family of forbidden witness supports \(\mathrm{Forb}_U(W) \subset \mathrm{FinSubsets}(E_W)\) and assigns to each forbidden support \(S\) the square-free monomial \(x_S = \prod_{e \in S} e\). The obstruction ideal is then

\[ \mathcal{I}_{\mathrm{Ob}}^U(W) \;=\; \bigl\langle\, x_S \;\big|\; S \in \mathrm{Forb}_U(W) \,\bigr\rangle \;\subset\; k[E_W]. \]
Theorem III.5.6C

Stanley-Reisner Obstruction Theorem

Lean: defined / proved
Statement
If \(W\) is in a square-free witness regime and \(\mathrm{Forb}_U(W)\) is a family of inclusion-minimal forbidden supports, the simplicial complex \(\Delta_U(W) = \lbrace T \subset E_W \mid \text{no } S \in \mathrm{Forb}_U(W) \text{ satisfies } S \subset T \rbrace\) is defined, and \(\mathcal{I}_{\mathrm{Ob}}^U(W) = I_{\Delta_U(W)}\): the obstruction ideal is the Stanley-Reisner ideal of \(\Delta_U(W)\). The lawful chart \(\mathrm{Flat}_U(W) = V(I_{\Delta_U(W)})\) is read as a coordinate subspace arrangement whose subspaces avoid the forbidden supports. If \(\mathrm{Forb}_U(W)\) is not minimal, reducing to inclusion-minimal supports yields the same radical obstruction ideal.

Corollary III.5.6D (Obstruction Invariants). In a square-free witness regime the following are read as obstruction invariants: minimal forbidden supports are minimal law obstruction patterns; faces of \(\Delta_U(W)\) are simultaneously admissible witness supports; links and restrictions of \(\Delta_U(W)\) are context refinements and boundary-local readings; and the minimal monomial generators of \(\mathcal{I}_{\mathrm{Ob}}^U(W)\) are the irreducible selected obstruction witnesses.

Example III.5.6E (Three-Witness Stanley-Reisner Chart). Let \(E_W = \lbrace x, y, z \rbrace\) and \(\mathrm{Forb}_U(W) = \lbrace \lbrace x,y \rbrace \rbrace\). Then \(\Delta_U(W) = \lbrace T \subset \lbrace x,y,z \rbrace \mid \lbrace x,y \rbrace \not\subset T \rbrace\) and \(\mathcal{I}_{\mathrm{Ob}}^U(W) = \langle x y \rangle \subset k[x,y,z]\). Similarly, \(\mathrm{Forb}_V(W) = \lbrace \lbrace x,z \rbrace \rbrace\) gives \(\mathcal{I}_{\mathrm{Ob}}^V(W) = \langle x z \rangle\). This chart is reused in the monomial law-conflict computations of Part V. In Lean, the chart is fixed as FiniteExamples.StanleyReisnerChart with obstructionIdeal_eq_span_xy and the face and non-face witnesses proved.

Law condition types

Principle III.5.8 (Law Condition Types). The general form of a law condition is a subfunctor of the functor of points \(h_X : \mathrm{CommAlg}_k \to \mathrm{Set}\) of the architecture scheme, \(L \subset h_X\); when isomorphism data must be retained — decomposition, refactor equivalence, semantic identification — the law condition is read as a substack \(L \subset \mathcal{X}\). The core of Part III reads closed equational laws as zero sets of ideals, and only those. The typology is:

Closed equational law
Read as the vanishing of an equation witness ideal \(I_i^E\).
Open / inequational law
Nonzero capability, enabled provider, available path: a \(D(f)\)-type open condition.
Constructible law
A Boolean combination of closed and open conditions.
Descent law
Local lawful sections must glue along a cover with gluing data.
Temporal / trace law
Relativized to runtime trace sheaves, state transitions, automata, and temporal predicates.
Higher / stacky law
Decomposition, semantic identification, refactor equivalence: groupoid- or stack-valued data.

The slogan of the canonical text is read accordingly:

closed law is equation.
general law is a geometric condition.
obstruction ideal is the closed defect envelope
of selected witnesses.

\(\mathcal{I}_{\mathrm{Ob}}^U\) collects the part of the selected witness family that is readable as a closed equational envelope. Open, constructible, descent, temporal, and stacky laws are handled as open subobjects, locally closed subobjects, descent conditions, trace conditions, or stack conditions once the required extra structure is fixed. This classification is a claim boundary: it fixes exactly where closed defects may be treated as ideals and refuses to force other law types into ideals unnaturally. How the closed equational vocabulary generates the coefficients and restrictions of obstruction is fixed as a theorem in the Law Equation Realization below.

Obstruction ideal sheaf

Definition III.6.1 (Local Obstruction Ideal). For a context \(W\), let \(\mathrm{Def}_E(W) = \lbrace I_i^E(W) \mid i \in K_E,\ \mathrm{role}_E(i) = \mathrm{required} \rbrace\) be the required equation witness ideals inside the observable presheaf. The local obstruction ideal is their sum:

\[ I_{\mathrm{Ob}}^E(W) \;=\; \sum_{i \text{ required}} I_i^E(W) \;\subset\; \mathcal{O}_E(W), \]

with the full-system ideal \( I_{\mathrm{Ob}}^{E,\mathrm{all}}(W) = \sum_{i \in K_E} I_i^E(W) \) kept distinct, so an optional failure survives as a stronger full-locus datum. Open, constructible, descent, temporal, and stacky laws stay out of this ideal sum; they live on the additional geometric data of Principle III.5.8.

Definition III.6.2 (Obstruction Ideal Sheaf). The obstruction ideal sheaf \(\mathcal{I}_{\mathrm{Ob}}^E\) is the image of the map \(\bigoplus_{i \text{ required}} \mathcal{I}_i^E \to \mathcal{O}_X^U\) from the direct sum of the required equation witness ideal sheaves; when the natural-language law universe is displayed, \( U := U_E \) and \( \mathcal{I}_{\mathrm{Ob}}^U := \mathcal{I}_{\mathrm{Ob}}^E \). Restriction compatibility, \(\mathrm{res}_i\bigl(I_{\mathrm{Ob}}^E(W)\bigr) \subset I_{\mathrm{Ob}}^E(W')\), follows from the restriction compatibility of \(\nu\) rather than being assumed, so the context-wise ideals form an ideal subpresheaf whose sheafification image is \(\mathcal{I}_{\mathrm{Ob}}^E\). Equation failure thus restricts coherently across local contexts. The same construction over all indices gives \(\mathcal{I}_{\mathrm{Ob}}^{E,\mathrm{all}}\) with the canonical inclusion \(\mathcal{I}_{\mathrm{Ob}}^E \subset \mathcal{I}_{\mathrm{Ob}}^{E,\mathrm{all}} \subset \mathcal{O}_X^U\).

Principle III.6.3 (Obstruction Is Ideal-Theoretic). An obstruction is not a failure label. The algebro-geometric condition of lawfulness is that \(\mathcal{I}_{\mathrm{Ob}}^U\) is zero, or pulls back to zero along a section.

Lawful locus

Definition III.7.1 (Lawful Locus). For the architecture geometry \(X\) and the obstruction ideal sheaf \(\mathcal{I}_{\mathrm{Ob}}^U\), the lawful locus is the zero set

\[ \mathrm{Flat}_U(X) \;=\; V\bigl(\mathcal{I}_{\mathrm{Ob}}^U\bigr), \]

so that \(x \in \mathrm{Flat}_U(X)\) exactly when all selected law witness ideals vanish at \(x\); that is, \(\delta(x) = 0\) for all \(\delta \in \mathcal{I}_{\mathrm{Ob}}^U\).

Definition III.7.2 (Ideal-Lawful Section). An architecture section \(s : T \to X\) is ideal-lawful for \(U\) when it factors through the lawful locus; equivalently, when \(s^{*}\mathcal{I}_{\mathrm{Ob}}^U = 0\):

\[ \mathrm{IdealLawful}_U(s) \;\iff\; s^{*}\mathcal{I}_{\mathrm{Ob}}^U = 0. \]

At this stage ideal-lawfulness is deliberately not identified with \( \mathrm{EquationLawful}_E(s) \) — the simultaneous vanishing of residuals is a different predicate, and their equivalence is exactly what Theorem III.11.1 proves under the producer theorems of the \(E\)-generated realization.

Principle III.7.3 (Lawful Locus Is Not Total Correctness). \(\mathrm{Flat}_U(X)\) is a zero set relativized to the selected equation system \(E\) (with its law reading \(U_E\)), coverage topology \(J\), witness family, and signature axes: \(\mathrm{Flat}_U(X)\) is lawful within \(U\). The flatness here is AAT-flatness — factorization through the lawful locus — and is distinct from the flatness of morphisms in ordinary algebraic geometry. It is not total correctness over all possible equation systems. AAT defines the lawfulness that can be spoken inside a fixed geometry.

Affine AAT charts

Definition III.8.1 (Affine AAT Chart). For a context \(W\), the affine AAT chart is

\[ \mathrm{AffAAT}(W,U) \;=\; \mathrm{Spec}_{\mathrm{AAT}}\bigl(\mathcal{O}_X^U(W),\, D_W^U\bigr), \]

the spectrum of the architecture coordinate ring on \(W\), where \(D_W^U\) is the decoration: typed coordinate labels, selected law readings, signature axis readings, the obstruction ideal, and AAT interpretation maps. \(\mathrm{Spec}_{\mathrm{AAT}}\) is not a new spectrum. It is the ordinary affine scheme \(\mathrm{Spec}(A)\) with decoration attached: \(\mathrm{Spec}_{\mathrm{AAT}}(A,D) := (\mathrm{Spec}\,A,\ \mathcal{O}_{\mathrm{Spec}\,A},\ D)\), using the ordinary topological space, prime ideals, and structure sheaf.

Definition III.8.2 (R-Valued Local Configuration Functor). For a context \(W\) and equation system \(E\) with law reading \(U\), the local architecture configuration functor is \(h_W^U : \mathrm{CommAlg}_k \to \mathrm{Set}\), where \(h_W^U(R)\) is the set of \(R\)-valued \(W\)-local architecture configurations: assignments of \(R\)-values to the typed coordinates readable on \(W\) that satisfy the structural relations. At this stage the selected law witness ideals are not required to vanish; the lawful locus is cut out afterwards by \(\mathcal{I}_{\mathrm{Ob}}^U\).

Ringed AAT topos and architecture scheme

Definition III.9.1 (Ringed AAT Topos). The sheaf-theoretic foundation of the architecture scheme is the ringed AAT topos \(T_X^U = \bigl(\mathrm{AATSh}(A,U,J),\ \mathcal{O}_X^U\bigr)\), with \(\mathrm{AATSh}(A,U,J) = \mathrm{Sh}(\mathrm{ArchCtx}(A), J_U)\). It is Part II's site-and-sheaf structure equipped with Part III's sheaf of commutative rings, and it is the base floor on which affine charts, the lawful locus, and derived intersections are placed.

Definition III.9.2 (Chart Compatibility). A family of affine AAT charts \(\mathrm{AffAAT}(W_i,U) = \mathrm{Spec}_{\mathrm{AAT}}(\mathcal{O}_X^U(W_i), D_{W_i}^U)\) is chart-compatible when at least the following hold:

Open immersion
Chart transition maps are open immersions of the underlying affine spectra.
Overlap representability
Overlaps are represented by affine AAT charts, typically localizations or the chart of \(W_i \times_W W_j\).
Restriction compatibility
\(\mathcal{O}_X^U(W_i) \to \mathcal{O}_X^U(W_i \times_W W_j)\) agrees with the restriction of coordinate rings.
Ideal compatibility
\(\mathcal{I}_{\mathrm{Ob}}^U\) restricts to the obstruction ideal on overlaps.
Decoration compatibility
\(D_{W_i}^U\) restricts to the selected AAT decoration on overlaps, including Atom labels, law readings, signature axes, obstruction ideals, and interpretation maps.
Cocycle condition
Triple-overlap chart transitions agree.
Locally ringed condition
After forgetting AAT labels, the resulting structure sheaf is locally ringed.

Definition III.9.3 (Architecture Scheme). An architecture scheme is a decorated locally ringed architecture geometry locally presented by chart-compatible affine AAT charts:

\[ X = \bigl(\,|X|,\ \mathcal{O}_X^U,\ D_X\,\bigr), \qquad D_X = \bigl(\mathcal{I}_{\mathrm{Ob}}^U,\ \mathrm{At}_X,\ \mathrm{Sig}_U,\ \mathrm{LawRead}_U,\ \mathrm{Flat}_U,\ \lambda_X\bigr). \]

Each point \(x \in |X|\) has a context \(W\) and a neighborhood \(N_x \cong \mathrm{Spec}_{\mathrm{AAT}}(\mathcal{O}_X^U(W), D_W^U)\), with chart overlaps satisfying Definition III.9.2. Under the forgetful reading that drops the AAT decoration, an ordinary locally ringed space with a scheme atlas remains. The underlying space \(|X|\) is not the space of the source itself; it is the underlying space of the architecture geometry relativized to the Atom vocabulary, equation system, and coverage topology. StandardArchitectureScheme is the strong object: decorated \(\mathrm{Spec}_{\mathrm{AAT}}\) charts glued by open immersions and the cocycle condition on the scheme side, preserving Atom labels, law readings, obstruction ideals, and signature axis readings on the decoration side.

Principle III.9.4 (Scheme Relativity). An architecture scheme is relativized to the source \(C\), the Atom vocabulary \(V\), the architectural equation system \(E\), the coverage topology \(J\), the coefficient ring \(k\), and the chart atlas. The same source generally yields different architecture schemes when any of these change: \(X_C^{V,E,J,k} \ne X_C^{W,E,J,k}\), \(X_C^{V,E,J,k} \ne X_C^{V,E',J,k}\), \(X_C^{V,E,J,k} \ne X_C^{V,E,J',k}\) in general.

Restriction of rings and gluing of schemes

Definition III.10.1 (Ring Restriction). A context morphism \(i : W' \to W\) gives a ring homomorphism \(\mathrm{res}_i : \mathcal{O}_X^U(W) \to \mathcal{O}_X^U(W')\), restricting coordinates on \(W\) to \(W'\). Under projection, a forgotten coordinate does not become zero; it merely ceases to be read in the restricted ring: 

forgotten coordinate != zero coordinate

Definition III.10.2 (Ideal Restriction). The obstruction ideal sheaf is compatible with restriction: \(\mathrm{res}_i\bigl(\mathcal{I}_{\mathrm{Ob}}^U(W)\bigr) \subset \mathcal{I}_{\mathrm{Ob}}^U(W')\). The reading of law failure restricts along context restriction. A projection may hide a defect from view, and that is all it does: 

unread defect != zero defect
unmeasured obstruction != vanished obstruction

Definition III.10.3 (Scheme Gluing). For a coverage family \(\lbrace W_i \to W \rbrace_{i \in I}\), a family of affine AAT charts \(\mathrm{Spec}_{\mathrm{AAT}}(\mathcal{O}_X^U(W_i), D_{W_i}^U)\) that is compatible on overlaps \(\mathrm{Spec}_{\mathrm{AAT}}(\mathcal{O}_X^U(W_i \times_W W_j), D_{W_i \times_W W_j}^U)\) glues — in the same sense as standard scheme gluing — to a locally ringed architecture geometry \(X_{\mathrm{glue}}\) in general. The glued object need not coincide with \(\mathrm{Spec}_{\mathrm{AAT}}(\mathcal{O}_X^U(W), D_W^U)\): in ordinary algebraic geometry, too, a scheme glued from affine charts need not be the Spec of its global section ring. Returning to a single affine chart on \(W\) requires explicit additional hypotheses: 

the glued underlying scheme is affine.
the canonical comparison X_glue -> Spec Gamma(X_glue, O_X) is an isomorphism.
O_X^U(W) identifies with Gamma(X_glue, O_X).
the glued decoration D_glue identifies with D_W^U.

Calling the result a StandardArchitectureScheme gluing requires not just section compatibility but the full chart compatibility of Definition III.9.2: open immersions, joint coverage, cocycle, and decoration compatibility. The sheaf descent of Part II reappears in Part III as the gluing of ringed geometry, and affine representability is an additional consequence of gluing, never an unconditional one.

Principle III.10.4 (Local Lawful Sections Need Not Glue). Independently selected local lawful sections do not by themselves yield a global lawful section; overlap compatibility and descent are required: 

forall i, exists s_i : W_i -> Flat_U(X)
does not automatically imply
exists s : W -> Flat_U(X)
with s|W_i = s_i

This local-global gap is the entrance to obstruction cohomology in Part IV.

Displayed equation source and the generated route

Theorem III.11.1 gives the correspondence between equation lawfulness and ideal vanishing. The next step strengthens it by one level: the equation system does not merely express the correspondence — its \(\nu\) family generates the obstruction coefficient and restriction, and its \(\epsilon\) family generates the displayed interpretation.

Definition III.11.3 (Displayed Equation Source). Fix the equation system \(E\) and a base context \(W_{\mathrm{base}}\). A displayed equation source is a finite index set \(D\) and, for each \(q \in D\), a selection of a local context \(W_q \to W_{\mathrm{base}}\), an architecture object \(A_q\), a required equation index \(i_q\), and a support atom \(a_q\). The displayed defect is not supplied as a field; it is defined from the equation residual, \( d_q := \epsilon_{W_q, A_q, i_q, a_q} \). Displayed equation fulfillment means \( \mathrm{EquationHolds}_E(i_q, A_q) \) for each \(q\). Because the required index and support atom are explicit on each chart, no empty law support and no free-floating defect observable can appear. For a cover-indexed source over \( \mathcal{U} = \{W_u \to W_{\mathrm{base}}\} \), \(D\) is the cover index set and the local contexts are the cover charts themselves — no second skeleton and no second chart-indexed equation choice is placed.

Counterexamples and non-conclusions

Counterexample (cancellation). Over a coefficient system without the no-cancellation discipline, a sum of witness coordinates can vanish while the individual witnesses do not — in characteristic 2, two present cycle witnesses sum to zero. This is why selected-family satisfaction is placed on the ideal, \(\mathrm{ev}_s(I_i^{E,\mathrm{sel}}) = 0\), not on a summed representative \(g_i = 0\) (Example III.5.5, Principle III.5.6).

Counterexample (gluing). Local lawful sections on each \(W_i\) do not force a global lawful section on \(W\) (Principle III.10.4), and a scheme glued from affine charts need not be the Spec of its global section ring (Definition III.10.3). Both failures are genuine geometric phenomena, not bookkeeping defects.

Non-conclusion (projection). A coordinate forgotten by projection is not a zero coordinate, an unread defect is not a zero defect, and an unmeasured obstruction is not a vanished obstruction (Definitions III.10.1 and III.10.2). Restriction limits what is read; it proves nothing about what is unread.

Non-conclusion (totality). \(\mathrm{Flat}_U(X)\) is lawfulness within the selected \(U\), \(J\), witness family, and signature axes (Principle III.7.3). Zero on a selected axis is not total zero, and no claim is made about unselected equation systems or unselected readings (Example III.3.3, Principle III.5.7).

Conclusion of Part III

Part III added a sheaf of commutative rings to the architecture geometry of Part II, defined the obstruction ideal sheaf from the symbolic violation coordinates of the required equations, and defined the lawful locus as its zero set:

\[ X \longmapsto (X, \mathcal{O}_X^U), \qquad \mathcal{I}_{\mathrm{Ob}}^U \subset \mathcal{O}_X^U, \qquad \mathrm{Flat}_U(X) = V\bigl(\mathcal{I}_{\mathrm{Ob}}^U\bigr). \]

AAT thereby acquires the tower

ArchitectureGeometry
  -> Ringed AAT Topos
  -> Affine AAT Charts
  -> StandardArchitectureScheme
  -> LawfulLocus

and its core reads:

law is equation.

lawfulness is vanishing.
the equation generates coefficient and restriction.
