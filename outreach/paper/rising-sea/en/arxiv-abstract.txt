Software engineering now has to judge machine-generated changes: which change preserves what, where the whole breaks, and how many choices remain. Judging them requires choosing what to read as structure and what to preserve. Starting from Atoms (typed primitive facts) and Laws (equations objects must satisfy), we fix this choice as a reading and develop architecture relative to a reading as one system, the foundations of Algebraic Architecture Theory (AAT). From finite Atom families we generate an operation-closed core and a geometry with site and coefficients; Law satisfaction becomes factorization through the zero locus of an ideal.
We prove theorems for five decisions. Gluing: the obstruction to gluing local states is a first Cech class whose vanishing is equivalent to the existence of a global state and of a global semantic repair. Diagnosis: evaluation-preserving changes of resolution, cover, and coefficients induce a map of diagnostic complexes, an isomorphism on first cohomology under a finitely decidable condition. Transport: transport along exact changes has a universal property; on squares generated from finite codes its exchange with base change is an invertible Beck-Chevalley comparison. Classification: a generated comparison factors into an invertible comparison and an idempotent normalization; observations decide preservation of a comparison exactly when their kernel lies in the comparison-preserving group, and compatible lifts form a torsor. Reconstruction: full geometries and all their structure-preserving morphisms form a category equivalent to one of local models given by primitive data and independent compatibility conditions.
Lens and protocol semantics are encoded in the system, yielding equivalence of laws, correspondence of morphisms, counting of operation-preserving changes, and unique extension from finite tables. Results correspond to Lean declarations.
