Now that AI generates software changes in large numbers, the central problem
of software engineering is to judge which change preserves what, where the
whole breaks, and how many choices remain. Such decisions require an
explicit choice of what to read as structure and which operations and laws to
preserve. Starting from Atoms, which are typed primitive facts, and Laws,
which are equations that objects must satisfy, the paper fixes this choice as
a \emph{reading}. It then constructs, as the foundations of Algebraic Architecture
Theory (AAT), the theory of architecture relative to a reading as one system. From a
finite family of Atoms we generate a core, closed under operations, and a
geometry equipped with a site and coefficients. The satisfaction of Laws is
expressed as factorization through the space defined by the ideal of the
equations.

Within this system we prove theorems for five kinds of decision: gluing,
diagnosis, transport, the classification of changes, and reconstruction. The
obstruction to gluing local states is represented by a \v{C}ech obstruction
class in degree one. Given an action of corrections and the sheaf condition,
the vanishing of this class is equivalent both to the existence of a global
state and to the existence of a global repair on the semantic side. For
changes of resolution, cover, and coefficients that preserve the evaluation of
Laws, we construct a map that carries diagnoses along the change. We give a
condition under which it induces an isomorphism on first cohomology, and show
that invariance for all families of Laws for which both resolutions are
adequate can be decided finitely.
We characterize transport along exact changes by a universal property, and
show that for squares generated from finite codes, the exchange of transport
and base change is given by an invertible Beck--Chevalley comparison. The
comparison of two construction routes generated from the same finite input factors into an invertible
comparison and an idempotent normalization factor, and its invertibility is
classified. For changes that preserve a comparison, we give a necessary and
sufficient condition for determining from observations alone whether a change
preserves the comparison, and a classification of all compatible lifts as a
torsor.

The culmination is reconstruction. The category of full geometries and all
their structure-preserving morphisms is equivalent to the category of local
models defined by primitive data and independent compatibility conditions.
This equivalence supports recovering the whole from local descriptions written
by separate parties.

The semantics of lenses (model synchronization) and of protocols, defined
independently of this system, are encoded in it. From this encoding we obtain the
equivalence of the validity of laws, a one-to-one
correspondence of semantics-preserving morphisms, the classification and
number of changes that preserve operations, and unique extension from finite
tables. The Lean declarations corresponding to the results of the text are
listed in the correspondence tables of the appendix. Together these results
give a foundation that treats the individual decisions, from local consistency
to reconstruction, as special cases of theorems about common objects.
